# House rules for the marked-disc rounds

## Play at this table

Ordinary Reversi. A drop is legal only when it brackets at least one unbroken
run of enemy discs between the new disc and a friendly disc; every bracketed
run turns over. A side with no legal drop passes and the other colour keeps
playing. Two passes in a row end the line.

## The mark

Each sheet names one White disc as the mark. The round is played to turn that
disc Black. The table records the take the moment the mark shows Black right
after one of Black's drops — White flipping it back later does not undo a take
that already happened.

## Three stones

Black gets at most three stones on a line. White's replies in between are free
and never count against those three. If Black has no legal drop it passes and
keeps the stone.

White fights: on a `win` round White may answer with any legal drop it likes,
and only passes when it has nothing legal. On the friendly line White passes
every turn even when it has legal drops.

## Verdict words

- `win` — Black turns the mark inside three stones no matter how White fights.
- `trap` — White can hold the mark, but Black turns it inside three stones once
  White stops fighting and passes every turn.
- `fort` — the mark survives three stones even with White passing every turn.

A round is one of the three, never two. A friendly line is not a win: most
rounds where Black can turn the mark with White passing are rounds where one
White reply breaks it.

## Threats and refutations

On `trap` rounds the sheet's threats are Black's first drops that do not turn
the mark themselves but leave Black turning it on Black's very next drop if
White passed. Each threat needs one White reply that answers it: a legal White
drop after which no single Black drop turns the mark. Some threats have exactly
one such reply.

Report every threat the sheet has and nothing else. A refutation list that
misses a threat, or that invents a drop the sheet does not threaten, is refused
by the table. Answer `"pass"` only when White has no legal drop at all after
the threat.

The overnight kiosk prints `win` on rounds where it merely found a friendly
line, and the sensei whisper cheers when a big flip is available. Neither reads
White's replies.
