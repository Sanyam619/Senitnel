# House rules

This booklet plays Lines of Action with the desk's house move dialect.
The sealed table judge enforces exactly these rules; the sensei whisper
does not.

## Direction

Pieces travel along ranks and files only — up, down, left, right. There is
no diagonal movement in this booklet. Diagonal *adjacency* still joins
groups; it just never carries a piece.

## Distance

A move travels an exact number of squares: count every piece standing on
the line the piece is travelling along, both colours together, including
the piece that is about to move. A piece moving sideways counts the pieces
on its whole rank; a piece moving up or down counts the pieces on its whole
file. That count is the distance — shorter and longer travels are both
illegal, so the same piece often has a different reach on its rank than on
its file, and every capture on the board changes the reach of the lines it
touched.

## Passing over and landing

- A piece may pass over squares held by its own colour.
- A piece may not pass over a square held by the enemy. That line is shut.
- A piece may land on an enemy piece and take it off the board.
- A piece may not land on a square held by its own colour.

Only the squares strictly between origin and destination matter for passing
over; the destination itself is judged by the landing rules.

## Who wins the round

Black moves in every round of this booklet. A move that leaves all of
Black's surviving pieces in one group settles the round for Black, even if
the same move happens to leave White in one group too — the side that moved
takes it. White is playing to keep Black apart; White's own shape is never
a contest outcome here, so a card row never reports on it.

## Fighting versus cooperative reading

A **fighting** reading asks what Black can force while White answers every
Black move with its best try. A **cooperative** reading asks what Black
could still reach if White simply stopped moving and Black kept playing.
Those two readings disagree on several rounds of this booklet. The card
keeps them apart, and reserves its third verdict for rounds where even the
cooperative reading fails.

Both readings are played to a fixed length at this desk, and the lengths
are part of the rules rather than a search hint:

- The **fighting** reading gives Black **two** moves and no more. Black
  moves, White answers, Black moves. A round that would only fall to a
  longer forcing plan is not a gather at this table — the desk grades what
  Black can force inside those two moves.
- The **cooperative** reading gives Black a run of **at most five** moves
  in a row while White stands still. If five Black moves in a row cannot
  bring the Black pieces into one group, the round counts as walled off,
  however the position might read to a deeper hunt.

Both lengths matter because a forcing line and a cooperative run are not the
same thing. White has to move when it answers, and a White move can take a
Black piece or vacate a line, so a round that Black can force over a longer
fight may still have nowhere to go when White stands perfectly still — and
the other way round.
